description: Papervine has two completely separate auth systems — platform auth for our customers and reader auth for their end users.

Papervine runs **two completely separate authentication systems**. They have different
users, different urgency, and different security surfaces. Keeping them apart is the most
important idea on this page — conflating them is the easiest way to over-build, because each
layer's hard problem is the other layer's non-problem.

- **Layer 1 — Platform auth** signs *our customers* into the Papervine dashboard. We are the
  identity provider here: we own accounts, organizations, and roles.
- **Layer 2 — Reader auth** gates *our customers' published docs pages* for *their* end
  users. We never run an identity provider for readers — the customer's own login system
  does, and we only verify a signed assertion it hands us.

## The two layers at a glance

| | **Layer 1 — Platform auth** | **Layer 2 — Reader auth** |
|---|---|---|
| Who logs in | Our customers (docs owners and their team) | Our customers' *readers* (their end users) |
| What it protects | The Papervine dashboard / control plane | Published docs *pages* (private, internal, or gated docs) |
| Who owns identity | **We do** — we are the IdP | **The customer does** — we only *verify* an assertion |
| Who needs it | Every tenant; a prerequisite for multi-tenancy | Only some tenants (typically enterprise) |
| Maps to the incumbent | Their dashboard account | Their "Authentication & Personalization" |

The asymmetry in the last two rows is why the layers stay separate. Platform auth is
table stakes — nothing multi-tenant works until a customer can sign in, create an
organization, and connect a repo. Reader auth is an opt-in feature most sites never turn on,
and it carries a much smaller security surface because the customer's identity provider does
the hard part.

## How the two layers are sequenced

Because the layers serve different needs, they ship in order of necessity rather than
together. Platform auth comes first and unconditionally; reader auth follows as customers
ask for it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="v1 — Platform auth only">
    Layer 1 lands first: [Better Auth](https://www.better-auth.com/) with its
    `organization` plugin, email/password sign-in (GitHub OAuth as a fast-follow), a
    middleware session check, role-based access control, and repo connection. The public
    renderer can go live as a single-tenant site *before* this — auth does not block getting
    online — but nothing multi-tenant works without it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="v2 — Reader auth, JWT first">
    Layer 2 arrives when the first paying customers ask for gated docs: the **JWT**
    handshake plus `public:` and `groups:` page gating. Per-user personalization (exposing a
    `user` variable in MDX) follows after that.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enterprise — SSO and scale">
    When a deal demands it: WorkOS SAML/SSO *into the platform* (Layer 1), the OAuth 2.0
    reader handshake (Layer 2), and per-user personalization at scale.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  These layers compose: an enterprise customer signs into the dashboard with Layer 1, and
  *configures* Layer 2 there to gate the docs their own readers see. The reader never touches
  Papervine's platform accounts, and a platform user never needs a reader session.
</Note>
