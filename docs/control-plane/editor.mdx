The **editor** is a three-panel workspace for editing a docs site directly in the dashboard:
an AI editing agent, the navigation tree, and a multi-modal MDX editor. It opens at
`/:org/:site/editor` (the **Editor** item in the rail, and **Open editor** on the site
overview).

<Info>
  The editor and the [authoring MCP](/features/mcp-servers#authoring-mcp-admin-write) are the
  **same write path**, not two parallel ones — both edit one server-side draft buffer on a
  working branch and publish through one backend. Whatever the agent does, you can do by hand,
  and vice versa.
</Info>

## The three panels

<Steps>
  <Step title="Editing agent (left, on demand)">
    A read/write assistant. It can search and read your docs **and edit them** — ask it to
    “rewrite the intro” or “add an FAQ entry” and it writes to the same draft you see. When it
    finishes an edit, the editor pane refreshes to show the change. It never publishes unless
    you ask.

    The agent panel is **hidden by default** so the page has room to write; open it with the
    **Ask agent** button (or <kbd>⌘I</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl I</kbd>) and close it from the button, the
    panel’s ✕, or the same shortcut. Your conversation is kept while it’s closed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Navigation (middle)">
    Your `docs.json` navigation, rendered from the **draft** of the current branch. Click any
    page to load it into the editor.

    Each group carries two controls — a **+** to add to it, and a cog that opens its settings
    (a page's cog opens that page's frontmatter). The **+** offers:

    - **New page** — name it, and Papervine creates the file and lists it in that group. The
      URL comes from the title. You land in the new page, ready to write.
    - **Add existing page** — for a page that exists as a file but isn't in your navigation
      yet. Only appears when there is one.
    - **New group** — an empty group nested inside this one, ready to fill.
    - **New tab** — a section across the top of your site. If your site doesn't use tabs yet,
      your current navigation becomes a first tab called **Documentation** and the new tab sits
      beside it; the dialog tells you before you commit to it, and nothing is lost either way.

    Everything here writes to your draft, so a new page and its navigation entry publish
    together as one change — and revert together too.

    **Drag to reorganize.** Each row has a grip: drag a page to reorder it inside its group or
    move it to another one, and drag a group to move it among its siblings. Drop a page onto a
    group's own row to put it in a group that has no pages yet. The grip is a button, so this
    works from the keyboard too — focus it, press <kbd>Space</kbd>, move with the arrows, and
    press <kbd>Space</kbd> again to drop.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Editor (right)">
    Edit the page in **Visual** mode (WYSIWYG — the page rendered, editable in place) or **Source**
    mode (raw MDX), and use **Diff** to compare your draft against what's published. Edits autosave
    to the draft buffer, and your chosen view **stays put as you move between pages**. Switching
    pages also **saves your in-progress edit first**, so a quick click away never loses what you
    just typed. Pages can be edited **together, in real time** — see [Real-time
    collaboration](/control-plane/collaboration).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## On a phone or a narrow window

Three side-by-side panels don't fit on a phone, so below roughly 1024px the two side panels
become **overlays** instead of columns: the editor keeps the full width, and **Navigation**,
**Files**, or **Ask agent** slide in over it. Dismiss an overlay by tapping outside it, by its
✕, or — for the navigation — just by picking a page, which closes it so you can see what you
opened.

Nothing is hidden at narrow widths; the panels are the same panels, they just take turns. At
desktop widths the layout is unchanged.

Controls that a mouse reveals by hovering — a page's settings cog in the navigation, the revert
icon beside a changed file in the Publish panel, a tab's drag grip — are **always visible on a
touch device**,
where there's no hover to reveal them. Nothing in Studio is reachable only by hovering.

## Links keep you inside the editor

Visual mode renders your real components, so the links in a page are real links — a markdown
link, or the `href` on a `<Card>`. Clicking one **moves the editor to that page**, the same as
clicking it in the navigation tree: your unsaved edit is written to the draft first, and you stay
in the editor.

Links are resolved against **your site**, not the dashboard, so `/quickstart`,
`../guides/components`, and `/quickstart#install` all land on the page a reader would get:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="A page in your site">
    Loads in the editor. Root-absolute and relative paths both work, and a `#fragment`, a query
    string, or a trailing `.mdx` is ignored when matching the page.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Anything external">
    An `https://`, `mailto:`, or protocol-relative link opens in a **new tab**, so your editing
    session is never replaced.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A page that doesn't exist">
    Nothing is followed — you get a “No page /… in this site” message. That's a broken link in
    your docs, surfaced while you're writing rather than after you publish.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A card you want to edit, not follow">
    A `<Card href=…>` wraps its body text in the link. Clicking that **text** puts the cursor in
    it so you can edit; clicking anywhere else on the card follows the link.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Editing tabs

A `<Tabs>` block in Visual mode is a **working tab strip**, not a preview of one. The bar sits
above the content and the tab you select is the one you edit:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Switch">
    Click a tab to show its content. Only the selected tab's body is on screen, so you edit one
    pane at a time — the same way readers see it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Rename">
    Double-click a tab's label, type, and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. That writes the `title`
    attribute on the `<Tab>`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Add and remove">
    **+** at the right end of the bar appends a new empty tab and selects it. The **✕** on the
    selected tab removes it — it's hidden on the last remaining tab, since a `<Tabs>` with
    nothing in it would vanish.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reorder">
    Each tab has a **grip above it** — drag that along the bar to move the tab. Its content moves
    with it, and the order you see is the order in the MDX. The grip is a button, so this works
    from the keyboard too: focus it, press <kbd>Space</kbd>, move with the arrows, and press
    <kbd>Space</kbd> again to drop.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Every one of these is a normal edit: it round-trips to MDX, autosaves to the draft buffer, and
<kbd>⌘Z</kbd> undoes it. Which tab is *showing* is not — that's just what you're looking at, so
it isn't saved and other people editing the page keep their own selection.

<Note>
  MDX has two ways to write a tab, and only one of them is editable as a strip. A `<Tab>` whose
  body sits on the **same line** — `` <Tab title="npm">`npm install`</Tab> `` — is parsed by MDX
  as inline content rather than a block, so Studio shows that block as labelled chrome with its
  source intact instead of an empty bar. Put a blank line around each tab's body and it becomes a
  strip:

```mdx
<Tabs>
  <Tab title="npm">

    `npm install`

  </Tab>
</Tabs>
```

  Either form renders identically for readers. Anything you insert from the `/` menu already uses
  the editable form.
</Note>

## Editing tables

A table is the one block that reads best *without* its structure showing — a published table is
hairlines and a bold header row — and edits worst that way, because nothing tells you where a cell
ends. So Visual mode draws the grid while you're in it, and the published page stays exactly as it
was:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Type through it">
    <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves to the next cell and <kbd>⇧Tab</kbd> back; Tab in the last cell adds a
    row. Dragging across cells selects them.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Add — or add several">
    **+** down the right edge appends a column; **+** along the bottom appends a row. Both appear
    on hover, and both take a **drag**: pull away from the table to add several at once, back over
    it to remove them again. A table never drags below one row and one column.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Select a column or row">
    The handles above each column and beside each row select it. That's a whole-column selection,
    so the formatting toolbar stays out of the way.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Remove one">
    A selected handle grows an **✕** — that deletes the column or row it belongs to. The table
    itself goes the way any block does, from the drag handle's menu.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The first row is the header, because that's what a markdown table is: `| --- |` under the first
row and nothing else. There's no separate header to turn on, and no column widths to set — GFM has
nowhere to write them, so a width you dragged would vanish on save.

<Note>
  **A cell can hold a list.** Markdown can't say that — a pipe-table cell is a single run of inline
  content — so Studio writes it the way MDX renders it, as HTML in that cell:

```mdx
| Feature | Notes                                         |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Auth    | Supports SSO<ul><li>SAML</li><li>OIDC</li></ul> |
```

  You type it as an ordinary list and it opens as one again next time; the HTML is just how it's
  stored. Emphasis inside those items survives the trip too.
</Note>

## Editing accordions

An `<AccordionGroup>` in Visual mode **is** the published component — the same border, the same
rows, the same chevron — with the editing controls handed into it, so what you're arranging is
what readers get:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Open and close">
    The chevron on a row really collapses it, so a long group stays readable while you work.
    That's a view of the page, not an edit to it: it isn't saved, it doesn't touch `defaultOpen`
    (which is what *readers* start with), and other people editing the page keep their own.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Name">
    The title is a field — click it and type. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to drop into the body, which
    is the order you write one in: heading first, then what's under it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Add and remove">
    Hovering a row reveals **+** and **✕** at its right end. **+** adds the next accordion directly
    below that row, with the cursor already in its name; **✕** removes that row, and is hidden on
    the last one — a group with nothing in it would vanish. A lone `<Accordion>` outside a group
    has neither: it's one block, added to and deleted like any other, from the drag handle.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Untitled is untitled">
    A new row has no `title` yet, so it writes `<Accordion />` rather than inventing a name you'd
    have to notice and change. The grey text in the field is a placeholder, not content.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Editing code groups

A `<CodeGroup>` is a tab strip too, and the tabs are the **fences' own titles** — what you name one
here is what the published page shows:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Switch and rename">
    Click a tab to show that block. Double-click its label to rename it, then press <kbd>Enter</kbd>
    — that writes the fence's title, so the tab and the page always agree.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Add and remove">
    **+** at the end of the strip appends a block, in the same language as the one you were looking
    at. The **✕** on the active tab removes that block; it's hidden on the last one. The **bin** at
    the far right deletes the whole group.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Language">
    The control on the right names the active block's language and changes it. Type to search — it
    knows the short spellings, so `ts` finds TypeScript and `yml` finds YAML — and a language it
    doesn't list is shown as written rather than rewritten.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Copy">
    Copies the block you're looking at, the same way the copy button does for readers.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

An empty fence says `// add code here` until you type in it. That's a hint, not content: it isn't in
your MDX and it never publishes.

Inside a group, the fence *is* the tab — so <kbd>Backspace</kbd> at the start of one and
<kbd>Delete</kbd> at the end of one do nothing, rather than merging it with its neighbour or turning
an emptied fence into a paragraph. Removing a tab is the **✕** on its label.

<Note>
  A fence's title is written either way real repos write it, and Studio reads and writes both:

````mdx
<CodeGroup>

  ```bash npm
  npm i papervine
  ```

  ```ts title="app.config.ts"
  export default { name: "docs" };
  ```

</CodeGroup>
````

  A block with no title falls back to its language, and a block with neither is labelled by
  position. Anything else already in the fence's info string — a line-highlight range like
  `{1,3-4}`, say — is kept exactly as you wrote it.
</Note>

## Editing cards

A `<Card>` in Visual mode is the published card, with the three things you author handed back as
controls:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Icon">
    The tile in the corner opens the icon set — search it, click one, or take the one that's there
    back off. It writes the card's `icon`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Title">
    A field, not a heading you have to find in Source. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to drop into the
    body, which is the order you write a card in.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Link">
    The **…** in the corner holds the card's link. A linked card is still a real link while you
    edit — clicking it opens that page in the editor — and clicking its title or icon puts the
    cursor there instead.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Remove">
    Also in **…**. A card in a group goes with it; the group itself goes from the drag handle,
    like any other block.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

An empty card says `Enter your card description here` until you type. That's a hint, not content.

## Badges and icons in a line of text

`<Badge>` and `<Icon>` are **inline** — they belong in a sentence, not on a line of their own — and
Visual mode treats them that way:

- A badge renders as the badge readers get, and its label is ordinary text: click into it and type.
- An icon is one thing: click it to change which icon, or select it and press <kbd>Backspace</kbd>
  to remove it. Inserting one from the `/` menu opens the icon set straight away, since picking the
  icon *is* the insertion.
- Both keep every attribute you wrote — colour, size, shape, and the `iconType` weight — and insert
  into the line the cursor is in rather than breaking the paragraph.

## Editing a file tree

`<Tree>` (also written `<FileTree>`) is built from rows rather than typed as MDX:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Add a row">
    **Add file or folder** at the bottom of the tree asks which, then puts the new row in with its
    name selected. A folder has the same **+** on its own row, which adds inside it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Name it">
    Every row's name is a field. That's the whole content of a `<Tree.File>` — there's nothing else
    to type.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Folder options">
    The gear on a folder holds what readers get: open by default, whether they can collapse it at
    all, and highlight. The tree doesn't collapse *while you edit*, since that would hide the rows
    you're arranging.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Remove">
    The bin on a row takes it — and, for a folder, everything under it — out.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Editing colour swatches

`<Color>` is a palette, and each swatch is a colour and a name:

- Click a swatch to edit it: a colour picker, the value as text (so `hsl(...)` or `var(--brand)`
  work as well as a hex), and its name. **Delete** removes that swatch.
- **+** adds one; **Variant** switches between the compact row and the table layout.
- A theme-aware swatch — `value={{ light: "#fff", dark: "#000" }}` — shows as its own MDX instead.
  That form can't be modelled as a single value, and showing you the source is better than
  silently keeping one of the two colours. Edit those in Source mode.

## The `/` menu

Type `/` anywhere in Visual mode to insert a block. Filter by typing, move with
<kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd> — the list scrolls to keep the highlighted block in view — insert with
<kbd>Enter</kbd>, and dismiss with <kbd>Esc</kbd>, which leaves what you typed in place, so
nothing is lost if you change your mind.

Anywhere means anywhere: the same menu, with the same blocks, opens inside a component — a tab
pane, a callout, a card, a step — and inserts into whichever one holds the cursor.

## Video and embeds

Two commands under **Media** in the `/` menu:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="/video">
    For a video your site serves. Give it a path (`/videos/demo.mp4`) or a direct URL, and the
    player appears in the page — playable right there in Studio, not a placeholder.
  </Card>
  <Card title="/embed">
    For YouTube, Loom, Vimeo, or any other iframe. **Paste the URL from your address bar** — the
    editor converts it to the provider's embeddable form, because a `youtube.com/watch?v=…` link
    won't play in a frame. A `?t=` timestamp is carried across.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

`/embed` asks for a URL and nothing else — the file lives on YouTube, so there's nothing to
browse. It names the provider it recognised before you commit, and **Add** stays disabled until
the link is one that can be inserted.

`/video` and `/image` open a **picker** instead, because those files belong to your site:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose one you already have">

    Everything the site holds of that kind, searchable by filename — including anything you
    uploaded earlier in this editing session but haven't published yet.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Or upload">

    **Upload** takes a file from your computer and puts it in your site's storage. Videos are
    MP4 or WebM up to 200MB; images are PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, AVIF or SVG up to 20MB. It lands
    in `videos/` or `images/`, named after the file you chose (lower-cased and hyphenated,
    because it becomes part of a public URL). Uploading a name that's already taken adds a
    number rather than overwriting the existing file.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish when you're ready">

    An upload is an edit like any other: it shows in the **Publish** panel's change list, can be
    reverted from there on its own, and goes live only when you publish. Until then it plays for
    you in Studio and is invisible to readers.

  </Step>
</Steps>

Where the file ends up depends on the kind of site. A [Papervine-hosted
site](/guides/start-from-scratch) writes it straight to your site's storage. A Git-backed site
**commits it to your repository**, so the file arrives alongside your MDX and is yours — the same
as if you'd added it by hand.

Both insert plain HTML rather than a Papervine-specific component — see
[Video and embeds](/rendering/components#video-and-embeds) — so the page stays portable, and video
written by hand or by another tool shows up as a real player here too.

<Note>
  One form deliberately shows its source instead of a player: a `<video>` with a `<source>` list
  has no single file to preview, so Studio shows the markup rather than guessing which encoding
  you meant. It renders normally for readers.
</Note>

<Warning>
  **Self-hosting?** Uploads go from the browser straight to object storage, so the bucket needs
  **CORS** allowing `PUT` from the host Studio runs on. The local MinIO in `docker compose`
  allows any origin already, so development needs nothing; a production bucket usually does.
  Without it an upload fails with a CORS error and the dialog says so.
</Warning>

## Editing steps

A `<Steps>` block ends its rail with a **+** button, in the place the next number would go.
Click it and a step is appended with the cursor in its **title**, because that's where a step
starts. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to drop into the body and keep typing.

Each step is two separate slots:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Title">
    The heading beside the number. It's a field, so click it and type — in a step you haven't
    named yet it shows a muted **Step title** prompt. Leave it blank and nothing is written to
    your MDX: an untitled step stays `<Step>`, never `<Step title="">`.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Body">
    Everything below the title — ordinary page content, so paragraphs, code blocks, callouts and
    the `/` menu all work inside a step.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The numbers renumber themselves as you add, and they're never written into your MDX — they're
counted at render time, so reordering steps in Source mode needs no renumbering either.
<kbd>⌘Z</kbd> takes a step back out.

## Task lists

`/task` inserts a checklist. Click a box to tick it, or press <kbd>⌘Enter</kbd> /
<kbd>Ctrl Enter</kbd> to toggle the item your cursor is in. A checked item is struck through.

It's ordinary Markdown underneath (`- [ ] todo`, `- [x] done`), which means one list can mix
checked items with plain bullets, exactly as Markdown allows, and a checklist written by hand or
by another tool opens here with its state intact.

Readers get the same shape: the checkbox stands where the bullet would, a finished item is struck
through, and a plain bullet in the same list keeps its bullet — so a checklist looks the same on
the page as it does while you're writing it.

## Deleting stops at a component's edge

Empty a tab and keep pressing <kbd>Backspace</kbd> and nothing further happens — it won't reach
back out and take the tab with it. The same at the other end: <kbd>Delete</kbd> at the end of a
component won't pull the next block into it. This holds for every component, not just tabs.

Removing a component is something you do deliberately: the **✕** on a tab, or **Delete** in the
block handle's menu. Selecting content and deleting it still works exactly as you'd expect — the
stop only applies to the single keypress that would otherwise escape the component.

Formatting inside the component is not part of that stop. With the cursor at the start of the
block that opens a component, <kbd>Backspace</kbd> strips that block the way it does anywhere
else — a list loses its bullets (including the first checkbox of a checklist), a quote loses its
bar, an emptied code block becomes an ordinary paragraph. It's the press *after* that one, with
nothing left to strip, that does nothing.

## Select All works outward from where you are

<kbd>⌘A</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl A</kbd> selects **the component you're inside**, not the whole page.
With the cursor in a tab it highlights that tab's text and brings up the formatting bar, so you
can bold or link the whole pane in one gesture. Press it again to widen a level — the `<Tabs>`
block, then any component around that, and finally the whole page. Nothing is taken away; the
document-wide selection is just no longer the *first* thing that happens.

This matters most in tabs, where the tabs you aren't looking at are off screen: selecting the
whole document and typing would replace content you can't see. It applies to every component
though — a `<Note>`, a `<Card>`, a `<Step>` — and in ordinary body text, where there's no
component to scope to, the first press selects the page as it always did.

## Preview is your real site, not an approximation

The **Preview** tab renders the page through the *same renderer that ships your docs* — the same
MDX compilation, the same components (`<Card>`, `<Tabs>`, `<Frame>`, `<HeroCard>`…), the same
theme. So what you see in Preview is exactly what readers get: what you see is what ships.

Editing happens in **Source** (raw MDX), and that MDX string is exactly what gets committed —
no conversion to a block model, no second source of truth. Git stays canonical. Preview reads
your current draft, so unsaved-but-buffered edits appear the moment you switch to it.

### Previewing the whole site

The Preview tab shows the page you're editing. The **Preview** button in the editor's toolbar
opens the **entire site** — navbar, tabs, sidebar, search — rendered from your draft, so you can
click around and check the things a single page can't show you: navigation order, how groups and
tabs read, whether links between pages land where you meant.

It opens as a **full-screen overlay on the page you're editing**, not in a second tab. Press
<kbd>Esc</kbd> or the ✕ to go straight back to where you were writing. Along the top: **Site
settings**, **Ask agent** (which closes the preview and opens the agent), and a **reload** for
when you want to see a change you made in another window.

Your pending edit is saved before the preview opens, so the last thing you typed is in it —
there's no need to pause and wait for the draft to save first.

It's your draft, not your published site, so nobody else sees it and nothing is live until you
**Publish**. Gated pages are visible to you here even if you don't hold the reader groups they
require — you're previewing your own site.

## Branches and the draft buffer

<Info>
  Branches and pull requests are **Git-backed features**. On a
  [Papervine-hosted site](/guides/start-from-scratch) there's no repository, so Studio shows
  no branch switcher and **Publish** is a single action straight to your live site. Everything
  else on this page — the draft buffer, the change list, per-file revert, discard, the agent —
  works identically on both kinds of site.
</Info>

The editor opens on your site's **deploy branch** — the one marked **Default** in the branch
switcher (top left). You edit it directly; the switcher also lists your open edit sessions and
can **create a new branch** for changes you'd rather land through review.

Wherever you edit, your changes buffer **server-side** per branch — they never touch the live
branch until you publish. Buffered edits persist across tabs, devices, and reloads, and the
agent and you share that one buffer. A clean session that you haven't typed into yet creates no
branch and shows the live content as-is.

Nothing is live until you publish.

## Publishing

The **Publish** button carries the draft to Git. Its action follows the branch you're on, and the
caret menu lets you pick either mode explicitly:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="On the Default branch → commit">
    Publish commits your edits straight onto the deploy branch. The normal sync then redeploys
    the site. This is the default when you're editing the Default branch.
  </Card>
  <Card title="On a working branch → pull request">
    Publish commits to the working branch and opens a PR into the deploy branch — review before
    it goes live. Use **Create new branch** in the switcher when you want this flow.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

On a [Papervine-hosted site](/guides/start-from-scratch) there is no repository and therefore
no third option: **Publish** writes your draft straight to the live site, and the caret menu
shows only the change list, per-file revert, and discard.

The outcome — a pull-request link, a commit hash, a plain confirmation, or an error — appears
as a toast that clears itself (and that you can dismiss).

If the deploy branch moved since you started editing, Publish stops with a conflict rather than
clobbering it — re-check-out to pick up the latest, then publish again.

<Note>
  The editor is gated behind the `editor.workspace` feature (admin-only while it’s dogfooded).
  Preview renders your draft through the real renderer on the fly; publishing surfaces the change
  through the normal deploy (or the PR).
</Note>
