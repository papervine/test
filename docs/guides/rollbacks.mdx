title: Rolling back
description: Restore a previous version of your site in seconds when a change goes wrong — no rebuild, nothing lost.
public: true
---

Something went live that shouldn't have. A broken link everywhere, a half-finished page, a
paragraph that named a customer. You want the previous version back, now — not after you've
found the right commit, reverted it, and waited for a build.

**Roll back.** Your site serves the previous version again within seconds.

## How to roll back

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the site's Overview">
    Every successful deployment is listed in the **Activity** feed, newest first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Expand the version you want back">
    Click a row to see its details — when it went out, who published it, how long it took, and
    which commit it came from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Roll back, and confirm">
    Within seconds, readers are served that version again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Rolling back is available to organization owners and admins.

## Why it's instant

Each deployment leaves behind a complete, unchanged copy of your site as it was at that
moment. Rolling back doesn't rebuild anything, re-read your repository, or undo a commit — it
points your site back at a copy that already exists.

That's also why it's safe. **Nothing is deleted when you roll back.** The version you're
leaving stays exactly where it was, so you can roll *forward* to it again the same way. A
rollback is itself recorded in the Activity feed, so the history shows who restored what and
when.

## After you roll back

Your site is now serving something older than its newest deployment, so the Overview shows a
banner saying so. It stays until a new deployment replaces it.

<Warning>
  On a Git-backed site, rolling back does **not** change your repository. Your default branch
  still contains the content you just rolled away from, and the next push will deploy it —
  replacing the rollback.

  Treat a rollback as the thing you do *first*: stop the bleeding, then fix the content properly
  and push. On a [Papervine-hosted site](/guides/start-from-scratch) there's no repository, so
  publishing from Studio is what replaces it.
</Warning>

## What you can and can't roll back to

A deployment offers a **Roll back** button when all of these are true:

- **It succeeded.** A failed deployment never went live, so there's nothing to restore.
- **It isn't already what you're serving.** Restoring the current version would do nothing.
- **Its copy is still kept.** The most recent 20 deployments per site are retained; older ones
  are cleaned up.
- **It's a live deployment,** not a branch preview.

Anything older than the retention window is gone for good, but its content still lives in your
repository's history on a Git-backed site.

## Rolling back one page instead

A rollback restores your **whole site** — every page, your navigation, and your images
together — to how it was at that deployment.

To put back a single page without touching the rest, use
[version history in Studio](/control-plane/editor#version-history) instead. That restores the
page into your draft, so you can look at it before publishing.
