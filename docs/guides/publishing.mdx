Papervine keeps your site in step with a branch of your repository. You write and push;
your docs update. This page covers what actually triggers that, how long it takes, and what
to check when a change doesn't appear.

<Info>
  This page describes a **Git-backed** site. A
  [Papervine-hosted site](/guides/start-from-scratch) has no repository, so none of these
  triggers apply to it — see [Papervine-hosted sites](#papervine-hosted-sites) below.
</Info>

## What triggers a rebuild

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="A push to your branch">
    The usual path. Pushing to the branch you connected triggers a build automatically —
    no action in the dashboard.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Connecting the site">
    The first build runs the moment you connect a repository.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A manual re-sync">
    The **Re-sync** control on the site's Overview forces a build from the current branch
    tip. Useful after changing a setting, or when a push was missed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Publishing from Studio">
    Publishing an edit in the [browser editor](/control-plane/editor) writes a commit,
    which triggers a build like any other push.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Only the branch you connected publishes. Pushing to other branches does nothing — which is
what makes it safe to draft in a branch and open a pull request against the published one.

## What a build does

A build reads the changed files from your repository, compiles your MDX, measures your
images, and rebuilds the search index — then swaps the result in.

Two properties matter to you as an author:

**Readers never see a half-updated site.** A build assembles the new version completely and
switches to it in one step. Nobody lands on a page that's half old and half new.

**Later builds are much faster than the first.** The first sync reads everything; after
that, only what changed moves. A typo fix is usually live in seconds.

## Watching a build

The site's **Overview** shows an [Activity feed](/control-plane/overview) with builds and
their status — building, live, or failed — updating on its own as the build progresses. You
don't need to refresh.

A failed build leaves the previously published version live. Your site never goes down
because a new build failed; you keep serving the last good version until a build succeeds.

A build that *succeeds* but shouldn't have is the other case — see
[Rolling back](/guides/rollbacks), which puts the previous version back in seconds.

## When a change doesn't appear

Work down this list — it's ordered by how often each is the cause.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check you pushed to the connected branch">
    Publishing follows one branch. A commit on `docs-update` won't appear on a site
    connected to `main`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the Activity feed">
    If the build **failed**, the feed says so and why. If no build appeared at all, the push
    never reached Papervine — re-sync manually and the problem is likely the repository
    connection rather than your content.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the page is in your navigation">
    A new file that isn't listed in `docs.json` renders at its URL but appears in no sidebar.
    See [organizing your docs](/guides/navigation).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Force a re-sync">
    Use **Re-sync** on the Overview. This rebuilds from the branch tip regardless of what
    webhooks did or didn't deliver.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rule out your browser">
    Published pages are cached hard so they load fast. A private window confirms whether
    you're looking at a stale copy of a page that did in fact update.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Automatic rebuild-on-push depends on the repository connection being fully authorized. If
  pushes never trigger builds but manual re-sync always works, that connection is what to
  look at — not your content.
</Note>

## Previewing before you publish

Two ways to see a change before readers do:

- **[The CLI](/cli)** — `npx papervine dev` renders your folder locally with the same engine
  as the hosted site; save and refresh to see a change.
- **A pull request** — draft on a branch, open a PR, and merge when ready. The published
  site keeps serving the connected branch throughout.

## Taking a page down

Deleting the file and pushing removes the page on the next build. To keep it published but
out of the way, drop it from your `docs.json` navigation — it stays reachable by direct link
for anyone who bookmarked it, which is usually kinder than a 404.

## Papervine-hosted sites

A [site you started from scratch](/guides/start-from-scratch) has no repository, so nothing
on this page about pushes, branches, or re-syncing applies to it. There is exactly one way
to publish: edit in [Studio](/control-plane/editor) and hit **Publish**. The change is live
on your site — there's no build to wait for and no pull request to merge.

Your live site keeps serving its previous content until a publish completes, and a publish
that fails leaves your draft untouched, so you can simply publish again.

To take a page down, delete it in Studio and publish.
